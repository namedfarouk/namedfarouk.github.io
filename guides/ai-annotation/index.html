Outlier

1. Sign up on Outlier

Signing up comes down to a few stages: set up your profile, upload your CV and verify your identity, then pass a short skill check. Take your time on the verification and skill stages, since that is where most people get rejected.

Stage 1: Create your profile

  • Go to the Outlier website at outlier.ai and click "Opportunities".
  • Choose Generalist skills, then English Writing. Pick the option that lists UK, USA, and Canada as supported countries.
  • Click "Sign up / Log in" and register with your full name, email, and phone number. An OTP is sent to your phone to verify it.
Tip: many people use an email dedicated to this work, kept separate from their main personal inbox. Optional, but it keeps things tidy.

Stage 2: Upload your CV and verify your identity

This is where most people slip up, so do not rush it.

  • Upload your CV or resume. Adding a LinkedIn profile is optional, not required.
  • Submit a valid government ID: driver's license, passport, or national ID.
  • Complete the face or video verification on the platform.
Use real, matching details everywhere. Any mismatch between your ID, your name, and your profile can mean instant rejection.

Stage 3: Skill verification

This stage decides whether you start earning, so treat it seriously.

  • Communication test: you answer a spoken question, which checks your English fluency, and you write a response, which checks your writing.
  • Be clear, simple, and confident. You do not need fancy vocabulary, just accurate, well-structured answers.
After you pass, timing varies. A project can drop immediately, within a few hours, or after a couple of days, so check back if your dashboard looks empty at first.

2. Aether and other assessments

Aether is Outlier's quality screener. It used to be called the Aether quality check, now it's the Aether quality screener, but the idea is the same. You're shown two responses, sometimes video, sometimes audio, sometimes image generations, and you have to rate and review them.

You usually get 30 to 50 minutes for this. That sounds like a lot until you're actually in it.

Here's what actually matters:

  • Don't copy and paste. It's one of the fastest ways to fail. Every answer needs to read like you actually thought about it.
  • Zoom and pan before you answer. If it's an image, zoom in on the details. If it's a video, don't just play it once and guess, watch it properly.
  • Study the generation before you write anything. Don't answer from a first impression. Look closely, then write.
  • Never guess. If you're not sure, look again. A confident wrong answer hurts you more than taking the extra minute to be sure.
  • Watch your spelling and grammar. Sloppy writing reads as low effort even when your judgment is right.
  • Use good internet. A slow connection during a timed assessment is how good annotators fail for reasons that have nothing to do with their actual ability.

Treat the assessment like the real work, because it's graded the same way the real work is.

3. Retaking Aether after a failed attempt

If you fail, here's the part nobody tells you clearly: you won't be able to create a Multimango account or start tasking until Aether is reset on your account.

The fix, in order:

  1. Check your Outlier homepage for a webinar section. Some accounts get served this after a failed attempt. If you see it, register and attend.
  2. Attending the webinar is usually enough to reset Aether. Give it a little time after the session ends.
  3. If Aether still hasn't reset after the webinar, then fill this form: Aether Screener Form
Important: that form is specifically for people who've already attended the webinar and still aren't reset. The form itself says not to fill it in if you've only failed once and haven't attended the webinar yet. Do things in that order, webinar first, form second, or you'll just be told to go attend the webinar anyway.

4. Setting up Hubstaff, Timesheet, and Multimango

Once you pass the assessment, go back to your dashboard and click Continue Tasking. You'll see a checklist of steps to complete before you're allowed to start. Two of those steps are creating a Multimango account and setting up your time tracker.

Multimango (your tasking platform)

  • On the checklist, you'll see Multimango credentials Outlier has already generated for you, an email and password.
  • Click Create a Multimango account. It opens multimango.com in a new tab.
  • Use the email and password given to you to create the account.
  • A 6-digit code gets sent to the email address you used when you created your Outlier account. You need that code to finish setting up Multimango, so check that inbox.

Hubstaff or Timesheet (your time tracker)

Outlier used to run everyone through Hubstaff. They're now moving accounts over to Timesheet, their own inbuilt timer. Some newer accounts get put straight on Timesheet, others are still on Hubstaff and will get migrated over time. Check the checklist step to see which one your account is asking for.

If it's Hubstaff:

  • Click Create on the checklist. It opens a tab asking you to accept Outlier's invite.
  • Accept the invite, then register your Hubstaff account.
  • Use the same email address you used to create your Outlier account. Don't use a different one, it'll cause linking issues later.

Your daily flow once both are set up

  1. Open Hubstaff and log in.
  2. Click the play or start icon to begin tracking.
  3. Go to Multimango and start working through your tasks.
  4. When you're done for the session, or stepping away, pause the timer. Press play again when you come back.

Your time tracker and your tasking platform are two separate things running side by side. Multimango is where the actual work happens. Hubstaff or Timesheet is just logging that you're working.

5. Quality tasking on Outlier

Passing the assessment gets you in. Tasking well is what keeps you in.

  • Be thorough, every time. Scroll up and down through the full task before you pick an option. Don't answer off a glance.
  • Rewatch video tasks more than once. One viewing is rarely enough to catch what actually matters.
  • Zoom in and move around on image tasks. Details hide in corners.
  • Always check the guidelines. Every project has them, and they shift. Don't assume the rules from your last task carry over.
  • Don't rush. Outlier doesn't put hard time limits on most tasks the way Handshake does, so use that. Speed is not the goal here, accuracy is.
  • Never use AI to write your answers. Don't copy and paste. Your responses need to sound human and specific, not generic. Reviewers can tell the difference, and it's one of the fastest ways to get flagged.

If you're choosing between fast and right, choose right. Outlier rewards accuracy over speed.

Quick tips for all three

  • The support widget opens with an AI assistant, not a person. Ask to be connected to a support agent first, then paste the fuller message.
  • Use the name your account is actually registered under. A name mismatch causes confusion.
  • Be calm and specific. State what you did, what you expected, and what went wrong.
Honest expectation: an "unapproved" flag often follows a quality review, and reinstatement is not always possible. Asking clearly still gives you the best shot, and at minimum tells you what to avoid on future projects.

Handshake

1. Sign up on Handshake

This is a detailed, step by step process for opening a Handshake AI account and qualifying for AI training jobs. It adds the critical technical steps drawn from official support documentation and community insights on X.

1. Initial access and sign up

Instead of the general Handshake homepage, use the dedicated fellowship portal so you are correctly funneled into the AI program.

  • Primary link: joinhandshake.com/fellowship-program
  • Action: click "Sign up now".

2. Account type selection

  • Existing users: if you already have a Handshake account through your university, select "Log in" and use your existing credentials. This links your student profile to the AI program.
  • New users, school is a partner: search for your school under "Find your school".
  • New users, graduate or school not listed: enter your personal or alumni email to proceed.

3. Profile and document completion

Unlike a standard job board, Handshake AI needs specific documentation to match you with frontier AI labs.

  • Resume upload: upload a current resume. Labs use this to match your subject matter expertise, such as STEM, humanities, or coding, to specific training projects.
  • Skills tagging: include specific skills like "Data Annotation," "Content Evaluation," or specialized domain knowledge such as "Python" or "Medical Writing".

4. Security and identity verification (critical step)

This is required before you can see any "AI Work" tasks.

  • Email confirmation: verify your email via the link sent to your inbox.
  • Phone verification: you may be prompted to verify a US based phone number via SMS.
  • Identity verification: Handshake uses a third party service. You will need a government issued ID (driver's license, passport, or state ID) and a live photo or selfie to match the ID.
  • This usually takes a few minutes to a few hours to approve.

5. Accessing the "AI Work" dashboard

Once your identity is verified, your dashboard switches from a standard job search view to the Handshake AI Dashboard.

  • Project matching: you do not apply to individual tasks. You are matched to projects based on your profile.
  • Onboarding: if a project is available, you will see an "Onboarding" or "Join" button. You must complete project specific training, usually asynchronous, before you can start earning.

3. Hedgehog and other assessments

Hedgehog is Handshake's generalist project, the same role Aether plays on Outlier. It checks your judgment the same way Aether does, but it's not just a screening hoop you jump through for free. Hedgehog is a paid project. You get compensated for the work you do on it, not just for passing it and moving on.

The same fundamentals that get you through Aether apply here: study before you answer, never guess, don't copy and paste, write clean and human, and don't rush into an answer you're not sure about.

4. Setting up Multimango

Multimango shows up on Handshake too, the same tasking platform used on Outlier, but it's not the same account. Your Outlier Multimango account is tied to an auto-generated outlier.ai email, and your Handshake Multimango account is tied to an auto-generated handshakecommunity.ai email. They're separate accounts on separate emails, even though you're using the same Multimango platform for both.

If you're coming to Handshake fresh, set it up the same way: use the credentials given to you on your dashboard, create the account at multimango.com, and grab the 6-digit verification code sent to the handshakecommunity.ai email tied to your account. Don't try to reuse your Outlier Multimango login here, it won't work.

5. Quality tasking on Handshake

The same quality standards apply here as Outlier: be thorough, check guidelines, never use AI, don't copy and paste, keep your answers human and specific.

The one real difference is time. Most Handshake tasks are timed, unlike Outlier, where you can usually take as long as you need. Keep an eye on the clock and aim to submit close to the time limit given for that specific task type. Don't rush so much that your quality drops, but don't sit on a task past its limit either. Getting a feel for how long each task type should take is something you pick up after your first few.
